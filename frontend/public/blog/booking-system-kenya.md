# Booking Systems for Kenyan Businesses: Do You Need One?

Salons in Kenya are walk-in. Barbershops are walk-in. Restaurants are walk-in.

But not every business should be.

If you are a tutor, consultant, auto mechanic, photographer, or event space — you need a booking system.

## What a booking system does

1. **Customers book online** — No more calling to check availability
2. **Automatic reminders** — SMS or WhatsApp reminders reduce no-shows
3. **Calendar integration** — Google Calendar sync for you and your staff
4. **Payment collection** — M-Pesa deposit to secure the booking
5. **Customer history** — See who booked before and what they wanted

## Do Kenyan businesses actually use booking systems?

Yes, but mostly the wrong ones:
- Google Calendar (you cannot take payments)
- WhatsApp (you cannot see availability at a glance)
- Paper books (you cannot send reminders)
- Phone calls (you forget who called)

A proper booking system built for Kenyan businesses solves all of these.

## How much does a booking system cost?

- **Simple booking + reminders**: KES 60,000–80,000
- **With M-Pesa deposit**: KES 80,000–120,000
- **With admin dashboard**: KES 100,000–150,000
- **Monthly maintenance**: KES 8,000–15,000

## Who should NOT get a booking system

- Salons with walk-in culture (unless they are high-end and appointment-only)
- Small mama mboga shops
- Hawkers and street vendors
- Businesses where 80%+ of customers are walk-ins

## Who SHOULD get one

- Tutors and coaching centers
- Consultants (business, legal, career)
- Auto mechanics and service centers
- Photographers and videographers
- Event spaces and venues
- Hair salons that are transitioning to appointment-only
- Clinics and private doctors

## Building a booking system for Kenya

When choosing a developer, look for:
1. M-Pesa integration for deposits
2. SMS/WhatsApp reminders (Africa's Talking or WhatsApp Business API)
3. Mobile-first design (your customers will book on phones)
4. Offline-friendly (internet drops in some areas)
5. Local support (someone you can call when it breaks)
